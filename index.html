What do these labels mean? — a quick reference tap to open
Envelope types — how a pot of money behaves
Flow — money in and out each month. Food, gas, everyday spending. A low balance at month’s end is normal.
Reserve — a sinking fund: fills up slowly for a big future cost (a car, taxes, medical), then gets drawn down when it hits.
Goal — like a reserve, but with a target amount and date you’re saving toward. Shows a progress bar.
Floor — protected money you don’t spend. Emergency. It sits there on purpose.
Project — temporary, for a one-off. Closes out when the project is done.
Categories — how envelopes are grouped on screen
Daily — the everyday envelopes you touch often: Food, House, Car Expenses.
Reserves — sinking funds building toward larger, expected costs.
Goals — specific things you’re saving toward with a target.
Giving — generosity: church, parents, long-term giving.
Retirement — long-horizon retirement holdings, shown apart from spendable money.
Long Term — savings meant to sit and grow, not for near-term use.
Categories are just labels for grouping — you can rename them or invent your own, and envelopes regroup automatically.
Account kinds — what an account is at the bank
Savings — a place money sits (most of our envelope containers).
Checking — the account bills are paid from.
Credit card — a card; charges get categorized and paid off.
Investment — brokerage, Roth, HSA — balances that move with the market.
A locked account (CD, retirement) just means the money isn’t meant to be touched right now — it’s information, not a warning.

Edits save when you leave a field. Changing an envelope’s owner or category only changes how it’s grouped — no money moves.
